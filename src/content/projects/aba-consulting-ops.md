## The arc

I joined ABA Consulting as a marketing analyst in January 2022 and left as a
senior advisor in January 2025. In between I was a finance analyst, then a
project manager, then part of the leadership running the organization itself.

Two jobs came with the last two roles. Deliver work a client would pay for, and
build an organization that could keep producing it with people who turn over
every year.

## Delivery

I worked on four client engagements, moving from analyst to running them:

- **T-Mobile** (2022). Customer and industry research on Gen Z brand
  recognition of their 5G product, built on a 1,100+ response survey. I
  contributed the research as an analyst; the team delivered six
  research-backed strategies and an executive recommendation deck.
- **IKEA** (early 2023). Market entry feasibility for financial services. I
  built customer segmentation models and visualizations from a 1,500+
  respondent survey to identify whitespace, and presented recommendations to
  IKEA stakeholders alongside 10+ expert interviews.
- **The Assistance Fund** (Jul to Dec 2023), as project manager. A 20-week
  growth engagement delivered on time, validated through a 3,000+ respondent
  survey and 15+ expert interviews, ending in a roadmap presented to client
  leadership.
- **Gates Foundation** (Jan to Jun 2024), as project manager. I built a
  quantitative partner-scoring methodology from 30+ peer-reviewed sources and
  10+ expert interviews, led 8 analysts across 5 workstreams, and delivered the
  recommendation directly to the Foundation's Manager of Strategy.

As a project manager I sourced clients, owned timelines, and led analyst teams
of 5 to 8 from scoping through executive delivery.

## Recruiting

Selecting analysts was the highest-leverage decision the organization made and
the least rigorous. Candidates were judged on impressions from short
interviews, which rewards confidence and polish rather than ability.

I owned the end-to-end hiring pipeline and recruited and interviewed **80+
Berkeley candidates** for analyst and project manager positions across two
recruiting cycles. I also built a structured scoring system that evaluated every
applicant against the same defined criteria, so candidates became comparable to
each other instead of being ranked on how memorable their interview felt.

## Training

New analysts arrived without the specific skills the work required, and every
project lead was teaching the same things from scratch.

I led professional development workshops for **80+ club members** on business
analysis, market research, and consulting frameworks, and separately built
internal sessions on data analysis, market sizing, and strategy frameworks plus
case competition prep. Those frameworks were adopted across engagement teams
rather than staying with mine.

That last part is what I care about. The material was used by people I did not
manage, on projects I was not on.

## Culture

An organization staffed by volunteers runs on whether people want to show up. I
organized social retreats and cross-club socials with other professional
organizations on campus, and organized and captained the club's competitive
intramural soccer team.

That work is easy to dismiss as soft. It is not: retention is what makes the
recruiting and training investment pay off, and people stay where they have
friends.

## What it taught me

Running delivery and running the organization behind it are different jobs, and
the second one is less visible. The engagements were the output. The hiring
pipeline, the workshops, and the reason people wanted to be there are what made
the next set of engagements possible without me.

That is the instinct behind most of what is on this site: build the repeatable
thing, not just the deliverable.
